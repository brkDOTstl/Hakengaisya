# Small staffing patterns

Choose after intake and deliverable analysis. Preserve [explicit reporting and upward acceptance](supervision.md) in every pattern. Titles alone grant no authority. The existing host may fill the root supervisor role; producer and accepting-supervisor contexts remain distinct.

| Pattern | Useful when | Work relationship | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coherent owner | One tightly coupled deliverable, including local design and checks | One supervisor assigns an adequate employee and accepts or returns its delivery | The supervisor verifies the user criteria |
| Parallel delivery cells | Separate deliverables have accepted inputs, disjoint writes and measurable schedule benefit | Employees submit to their team lead; each lead accepts its scope and submits a real integrated delivery to its parent | The root supervisor accepts the integrated user result |
| Temporary specialist | A bounded technical unknown blocks otherwise adequate implementation | The responsible supervisor assigns a specialist finding and accepts it as input for implementation | The scoped uncertainty is resolved or explicitly blocked; specialist status grants no peer signoff authority |

Stable artifact interfaces favor low-interaction handoff. Temporary close collaboration is appropriate for an unresolved interface, with a clear decision/output and a stopping condition. Tool wrappers and the optional evaluator can provide reusable capabilities without creating a model that continuously manages them. Do not ask an underqualified worker to learn through unlimited failed attempts.

Count useful owner contexts, integration work, independent review, startup and handoff cost. The planner can compare bounded pool alternatives; it cannot validate the semantic quality of the decomposition. A specialist and a reviewer may require separate contexts even when no execution overlaps. A large graph does not by itself justify management tiers.
